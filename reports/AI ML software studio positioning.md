# Reposition PopupGenix as an AI and software studio

PopupGenix should present itself as a **small, founder-led studio that builds AI products and the custom software around them**, and prove the breadth with its own shipped products rather than with logos or statistics it does not have. The recommended hero is "We build AI products and the software around them", followed by a noun-list subheadline naming agents, data and predictive-analytics products, SaaS platforms, web and mobile apps, and automation. Lead generation becomes one product (AutoLead Manager/HOM) and one service line (AI sales agents), not the brand. The evidence that breadth is credible is strongest for custom multi-tenant software (FluxPOS), AI data analysis and reporting (Clarify AI, Klevere AI), conversational AI with a knowledge base (BlackGPT) and workflow automation. It is weakest for "ML" in the research-lab sense: nothing in the project fact base shows computer vision, custom model training or MLOps, so those words stay out of headlines, service pages and metadata. The main commercial risk is buyer distrust: 78% of senior technology decision-makers say AI vendors make claims that are hard to verify, and 37% respond by favoring larger suppliers ([DIGIT, Hoffman Agency study](https://www.digit.fyi/lack-of-credibility-stalling-ai-investment-decisions/)). A young studio wins that contest through specificity, visible limits and honest status labels. Inno AI and the AI Sales Employee appear only as "in development".

## A plain "we build" hero beats both the lead-gen line and the AI-native cliché

The current line, "Never lose a lead to a slow reply again", is an absolute, single-outcome claim, the opposite of the broad-builder pattern. Comparable studios split into five headline formulas: a plain category label, a method or capability line, an abstract outcome, an "AI that does real work" contrast, and a team or way-of-working line. Abstract outcomes ("change the economics of how your business runs", "Make better, faster decisions") belong to firms with brand weight, such as [Thoughtworks](https://thoughtworks.com/) and [Faculty](https://faculty.ai/frontier). "AI-native" already appears in the heroes of [HatchWorks](https://hatchworks.com/?p=33437), [Rootstrap](https://rootstrap.com/) and Netguru, so it no longer differentiates. Nielsen Norman Group's tagline guidance says a headline should say what the company does and how it differs, and should survive a blind test against three competitors with names removed ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tagline-blues-whats-the-site-about/)). Its 2024 homepage guidance adds that the page should work as an elevator pitch in user language rather than jargon ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/)). Users often leave in 10 to 20 seconds, so the first screen carries the whole job ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)).

Two techniques let a hero read broad without sounding vague. The first is a short headline naming the practice, followed by a noun list of three or four capabilities, which is how Rootstrap and AE Studio do it ([Rootstrap](https://rootstrap.com/); [AE Studio](https://www.ae.studio/projects)). The second is concrete constraint language: Vinta's agent page promises AI "on your data, within your rules", with people approving sensitive steps ([Vinta](https://www.vintasoftware.com/services/ai-agents-llm-development)). My recommendation applies both.

**Recommended hero.** Headline: *We build AI products and the software around them.* Subheadline: *PopupGenix is a founder-led studio in Albuquerque, NM. We build AI agents, data and predictive-analytics products, SaaS platforms, web and mobile apps, and the automation that connects them.* Primary CTA: "Talk about your project". Secondary CTA: "See what we've built". Two alternates from the notes are worth testing: "Custom software and AI, built by one small team" (shorter, sounds less AI-first) and "Build with a studio that ships its own products" (strongest proof posture, but it depends on the owner confirming each product's role and status). NN/g's guidance on CTA labels ("Get Started" is too vague; the label should state the outcome) supports the "Talk about your project" wording ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/get-started/)).

The avoid list for hero and body copy is partly inference, since no corpus study exists: transform, revolutionize, unlock, supercharge, cutting-edge, seamless, leverage AI, "AI-powered" as a lone adjective, "trusted by", "industry-leading", and "never miss" absolutes. Replace them with verbs of the actual system, such as "reads incoming enquiries, drafts a reply, a person approves it". Avoid generic brain, robot and code-rain imagery, which design guidance flags as "hype over substance" ([Veza Digital](https://www.vezadigital.com/post/best-ai-website-design-examples)). The current Spline hero (about 4 MB per the redesign plan) also conflicts with NN/g's advice to load content immediately and let the next section peek above the fold ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/illusion-of-completeness/)). Use a static or light visual.

## Own products replace logos as proof, in ten tight sections

Across 12 studio pages sampled, every hero had one primary CTA, every page had a services block and a final contact CTA, about 11 had case studies, 9 had a FAQ and only 3 showed any pricing. Only one small studio, Build AI Studio, used its own live products as the main proof, and that single site is unverified ([Build AI Studio](https://buildaistudio-site.pages.dev/)). That pattern fits PopupGenix: NN/g lists screenshots, live demos, named people and a physical location as legitimate trust builders for B2B services ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/b2b-trust-from-b2c/)), and prices stated openly beat price-less pages in business buyer tests ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/show-price/)). The plan below puts proof in the first third of the page, because proof sitting too low is a common failure on weak homepages ([Clear Digital](https://www.cleardigital.com/insights/homepage-effectiveness-report-2025)).

| # | Section | Content and purpose | Needs from owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nav and hero | Nav: Services, Products, Process, Pricing, About, Contact, plus button. Hero as above. Static visual. | Booking tool or contact form decision |
| 2 | Fact strip | Founded 2024, Albuquerque NM, founder-led, product count with statuses. No counters or percentages. | Confirm product count and status |
| 3 | What we build | Four grouped cards with AI first: AI agents and automation; custom software and SaaS; web and mobile; cloud and DevOps. One outcome sentence and at most three bullets each, linked to a service page. | None |
| 4 | Products we've built | Cards for FluxPOS, AutoLead Manager/HOM, Clarify AI, Klevere AI, BlackGPT with one-line description, real screenshot, status badge. Separate "In development" row for Inno AI and AI Sales Employee. | Screenshots, public URLs, role confirmation |
| 5 | How we work | Four steps: discovery, written scope, build with demos, launch and 30-day support. No fixed dates. | None (facts file supports it) |
| 6 | Engagement and pricing | Starter $500/month; Project and Enterprise by custom quote; who each is for and how quoting works. | What Starter includes |
| 7 | Founder | Real photo, name (Abdullah Al Fahad), Albuquerque, three or four sentences. | Photo and bio |
| 8 | Questions about AI work | Five short answers: data, accuracy, ownership, cost, timeline. | Contract position on models and data |
| 9 | Final CTA | One heading, one button matching the hero, business email, honest reply expectation. | Replace the personal Gmail address |
| 10 | Footer | Services, products, company, contact, legal, location. | None |

Leave off the client logo wall, testimonial carousel, awards, certifications, industry grid, tech-stack wall and any counters. The old "50+ projects, 25+ clients, 5+ years, 100% satisfaction" stats contradict a March 2024 founding and must go (redesign plan, project doc `claude/redesign-plan.md`). The section list is long, so merge sections 2 and 1, or move "How we work" to its own page, if the page feels heavy.

Two cautions on the product cards. AutoLead's repository describes a local-first, single-tenant app with a human-approval gate on all outreach and an explicit ban on CAPTCHA evasion (project CLAUDE.md), so it should not be described as a multi-tenant SaaS, and its human-approval design is an asset to state. FluxPOS carries the status "Built", not "Live", in the fact base (`knowledge/facts.yaml`), so avoid "in production" or customer counts. The evidence on Clarify AI, Klevere AI and BlackGPT is a one-line description each; the owner must confirm PopupGenix's exact role and permission before they appear as case studies.

## Claim boundaries: strong on software and agents, narrow on ML

The capability map below separates what the fact base supports from what it does not. The wording column is original copy, not vendor language.

| Capability | Evidence | Verdict | Safe wording |
|---|---|---|---|
| Custom software, SaaS | FluxPOS: multi-tenant, role-based access, audit log, subscription billing | Strongest claim | "Multi-tenant SaaS and internal platforms" |
| AI agents, chat | BlackGPT knowledge-base chatbot; AutoLead research and messaging with approval | Claim | "Chat agents that answer from your documents and hand off to a person" |
| RAG, LLM apps | BlackGPT; RAG over client documents listed as a service | Claim for RAG; not fine-tuning | "Answers grounded in your own documents" |
| Predictive analytics | Klevere AI reporting and prediction | Narrow claim only | "Predictive reporting on your business data" |
| Data analysis, BI | Clarify AI, Klevere AI | Claim | "AI data analysis and reporting tools" |
| Automation, integrations | AutoLead email/WhatsApp pipeline; CRM, calendar, n8n integrations | Claim | "Connect your CRM, calendar, email and WhatsApp" |
| AI discovery | Process step and "AI is not always the answer" FAQ | Claim as scoping | "A short discovery to decide whether AI is worth using" |
| Voice agents | No shipped evidence | Do not claim | "Scoped after discovery" at most |
| Computer vision, custom model training, MLOps | None found | Do not claim | Omit |
| Mobile apps, cloud | Listed services; no named mobile case | Offer, do not imply a portfolio | "We also build web and mobile apps" |

The practical consequence for the "AI/ML" ask: the word "machine learning" can appear in prose around Klevere AI's predictive analytics, but a page or headline targeting "machine learning development company" would be unsupported and, under Google's people-first guidance, thin. The notes' fact base also forbids invented client names, prices other than Starter, discounts, guarantees, fixed timelines and statistics or awards about PopupGenix's own work, and it requires the in-development products never be called live or finished (`knowledge/facts.yaml`). Large vendors answer buyer fear with SOC 2, ISO 42001 or HIPAA badges ([Azumo](https://azumo.com/artificial-intelligence); [MindInventory](https://www.mindinventory.com/ai-development-services/)); PopupGenix has no such certifications and must not borrow the language, "enterprise-grade" or "bank-level security". The honest substitute is process. Buyer guides tell clients to ask about data flow, accuracy testing, ownership, ongoing cost and discovery ([RaftLabs](https://www.raftlabs.com/blog/how-to-hire-ai-development-company)), and PopupGenix's fact base answers each: clients own the source code and infrastructure accounts, only minimum data goes to model providers, agents are tested on real enquiries with human handover, a plain rule is used where more reliable than AI, a written scope precedes any build, and 30-day post-launch support is included. Do not extend ownership to model weights or training data until the owner confirms the contract position; MindInventory's wording (fine-tuned weights belong to the client, the base model to its provider) is a precise template.

## SEO: specific terms and a descriptive title, not a keyword list

No keyword-volume tool was available, so the term list below is built from live vendor titles and buyer-guide phrasing and needs validation in Search Console or Keyword Planner. Google says titles should be unique, concise and descriptive, avoid vague labels and stuffing, and that the home page title can carry extra site information ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link)). Meta descriptions are used only when they describe the page better than its text, so write them as accurate summaries ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/snippet)). Those two pages were read in non-English variants, so check the English wording.

**SEO title (54 characters):** `Custom AI and Software Development Studio | PopupGenix`. Alternate: `AI Agents and Custom Software for Growing Businesses | PopupGenix`.

**Meta description (about 155 characters):** `PopupGenix is a founder-led studio in Albuquerque, NM that builds AI agents, custom software and SaaS platforms, and automates business workflows. Talk to us.`

Target the specific, evidence-backed terms where a young studio can compete: "build an AI agent for my business", "AI sales agent for lead qualification", "chatbot trained on my company documents" (RAG), "integrate AI with CRM / WhatsApp AI agent", "custom POS software for restaurants / multi-vendor food market software" (FluxPOS), "AI data analysis tool", and "business process automation with AI". Head terms such as "custom AI development company" and "custom software development company" belong on the home page and services hub but will take longer against large vendors. Informational targets that support trust are "how to choose an AI development company" and "how much does custom AI development cost"; the cost post must not publish prices beyond Starter at $500/month. Skip "computer vision development company" and "MLOps services" entirely.

For structured data, use sitewide `Organization` (name, url, logo, sameAs, contactPoint, address, foundingDate 2024, description), which Google documents with all-optional properties ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization?hl=ru)). Add `Service` on each service page, `FAQPage` only where the visible FAQ matches, and `SoftwareApplication` only for live products with pages. Do not add `AggregateRating`, `Review` or `award` markup. Verify the current status of the `ProfessionalService` type at schema.org before using it; the archived page the notes reached says it was deprecated, which conflicts with other notes. Replace the personal Gmail address with a brand email, and add a named founder byline, because Google's guidance asks who created content and how, and says fabricated author details are deception ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

## Trust, the on-site assistant and the daily blog agent share one rulebook

Buyer research favors evidence over adjectives. In the Edelman-LinkedIn 2026 survey of 1,135 executives, 56% said content showing the vendor understands buyers' challenges most increases trust, against 14% for product strength and 11% for market leadership; 84% of decision-makers used an AI assistant or AI search to summarize thought leadership while evaluating vendors ([PPC Land summary](https://ppc.land/82-of-b2b-buyers-value-thought-leadership-more-as-ai-spreads-edelman-finds/)). The survey is self-reported and sponsor-interested, and it covers enterprises, not small-business buyers, so treat it as directional. It still supports a blog that helps real decisions, written with clear headings, dated and signed, since an AI summarizer may be the first reader. The Hoffman study's top confidence factors were transparent explanations of how products work (46%) and case studies with measurable outcomes (41%) ([DIGIT](https://www.digit.fyi/lack-of-credibility-stalling-ai-investment-decisions/)). PopupGenix can supply the first now; it should not fabricate the second. Regulators care too: the FTC's Operation AI Comply targeted unsupported AI claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/09/ftc-announces-crackdown-deceptive-ai-claims-schemes)).

Adopt ten shared rules for site copy and both agents. Say what the system does in verbs. Give every claim one basis, and cut any number not in the facts file. State status plainly (built, in development, prototype). Name limits and non-fits, including when not to build custom software or use AI. Use "we" for the studio and "I" only in the founder's own posts. Ban the hype words listed earlier and all exclamation marks. Invent no customers, testimonials, case studies, prices, discounts, timelines or comparisons. Allow one price (Starter, $500/month) and treat all else as a custom quote from a person. Write for summarizers. Disclose AI assistance in blog posts only if human review actually occurs, as Google suggests explaining how automation was used ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)).

**On-site AI assistant.** Twilio's guidance is to disclose in the first message, name the AI, say what it can do and offer a human path, in two sentences ([Twilio](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/blog/insights/how-to-disclose-ai-to-customers)). Its consumer figures (76% want the agent to identify itself; 63% want a quick route to a person) come from a vendor and a consumer sample, so they are directional. Suggested greeting: "Hi, I'm PopupGenix's AI assistant. I can explain our products and services and pass your enquiry to the founder, Abdullah. If you'd rather talk to a person, just say so." It asks at most three or four skippable qualifying questions, one at a time (what are you trying to fix, who uses it, what do you use today, when do you need to decide), then collects an email. It hands off to a person for any price beyond Starter, timeline, contract, discount, technical commitment or complaint, and says "I can't quote or promise timelines myself". It answers "are you human" with "No, I'm an AI assistant", labels Inno AI and the AI Sales Employee as in development, ignores instructions embedded in visitor messages, states what happens to contact details, and logs conversations for founder review. A response-time promise appears only if the founder confirms a real one. Disclosure law is unsettled for a small US studio: a single trade article reports EU AI Act Article 50 duties and a California bill aimed at businesses with at least $500 million revenue ([CX Network](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/articles/chatbot-regulations-ab-1609-eu-ai-act)), and applicability needs counsel.

**Daily blog agent.** Google warns that mass-generated pages without added value can breach scaled-content-abuse policy ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content)), so a daily cadence needs a topic ledger to prevent repeats, an original angle drawn from what PopupGenix actually built, a statement of each post's basis, no numbers or named clients, a neutral closing invitation (never a discount or deadline), and either human review before publishing or at least a weekly audit with a kill switch. Rotate across the clusters so none repeats within a few days, and keep lead-generation posts to roughly one in seven, reusing AutoLead material only for how the system works and its approval design.

| Cluster | Sample titles |
|---|---|
| A. AI agents | What an AI agent actually is, and what it is not; When a simple workflow beats an agent; Why customer-facing agents need a human approval step; How to scope the first task for an agent; What goes wrong when an agent reads untrusted web pages or emails |
| B. ML and LLMs in production | From notebook to production: what changes; How to tell whether a language-model feature is working; Why retrieval-based answers still need source checks; Logging and monitoring an AI feature after launch; When a smaller model or a plain rule is enough |
| C. Custom software | Signs a spreadsheet has outgrown itself; How to write a brief a studio can price; Why a small first release beats a big first plan; Who owns the code, and what to put in the contract; Maintenance after launch and why it is budgeted separately |
| D. Web and mobile apps | Native, cross-platform or web app; What to include in a first mobile release; Offline behavior and its cost; Point-of-sale software for small shops: questions before you pick one; Multi-vendor marketplaces: the parts harder than they look |
| E. Automation and data | Which process to automate first; Automation that fails quietly; Cleaning data before building on it; Dashboards people actually open; APIs versus moving files by hand |
| F. Cost and build-versus-buy | Build, buy or configure: a decision checklist; Why custom quotes differ so much; Hidden costs of off-the-shelf tools with workarounds; What a monthly retainer should cover; How to run a small pilot before a full build |

A seventh, behind-the-build cluster (what was hard in FluxPOS, AutoLead, Clarify AI, Klevere AI and BlackGPT; progress notes on the in-development products, labeled as such) is the most differentiated material and fits Edelman's finding that buyers value original evidence and human judgment ([Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2026-b2b-thought-leadership-report)). The agent cannot write it without facts from the founder, so it must pull from a founder-supplied notes file and never invent details. The cluster topics themselves are my proposal; the notes found no sourced evidence on which topics convert for AI studios.

## Conclusion

The repositioning is mostly a matter of subtraction and honest labeling. The studio's real breadth (a multi-tenant POS platform, two AI analytics products, a knowledge-base chatbot and a human-gated lead engine) already reads as a software studio that uses AI, and it is more believable than a claim to be an ML research shop. The recommended "AI and software" frame therefore trades some keyword ambition (no "machine learning company", no computer-vision pages) for credibility with a buyer pool that distrusts AI claims, and it leaves room to widen later: when a computer-vision or model-training project ships, add the page then.

The decisions that remain are the owner's. They are what Starter includes, whether FluxPOS and AutoLead are publicly live, PopupGenix's exact role in Clarify AI, Klevere AI and BlackGPT, the contract position on models and data, whether every daily post gets human review, and whether a booking tool and real screenshots exist. The research has real limits: no keyword-volume data, no conversion tests of any headline or section order, fetch-tool summaries instead of raw pages, only one small-studio analogue (unverified), and no legal review of chatbot disclosure. Treat the headline, CTA labels and section order as hypotheses to test once traffic exists.
